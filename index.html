About

My work sits at the intersection of protocol design and operational reality. I plan networks that survive link failures gracefully — sub-200ms convergence isn't a spec, it's a baseline requirement when you're carrying voice and financial trading traffic. And I build the automation and monitoring that keeps those networks observable long after the design documents are filed away.

Currently based in Singapore, consulting independently on backbone redesigns and SD-WAN transitions for enterprise clients moving off legacy WAN architectures.

Projects

Multi-Region MPLS Core Redesign

Meridian Telecom

Problem: Legacy hub-and-spoke MPLS topology created single points of failure and suboptimal traffic paths. Convergence during PE/P node failures exceeded 1 second, triggering voice quality degradation for enterprise VPN customers across 5 countries.

Approach: Redesigned as a dual-plane topology with TI-LFA fast reroute. Implemented segment routing to simplify label management and enable traffic engineering without RSVP-TE state overhead. Deployed gNMI-based telemetry streaming to replace SNMP polling, giving the NOC real-time path visibility during convergence events.

Result: Convergence time reduced to under 200ms. Traffic engineering policies reduced inter-region latency by 15ms on the Singapore–Tokyo path. Eliminated 2 hours per week of manual traffic steering during maintenance windows.

Enterprise SD-WAN Migration

Confidential — Financial Services

Problem: Client operated 42 branch offices across 6 countries on dedicated MPLS L3VPN circuits, paying $380K/month in WAN circuit costs. Application performance monitoring was minimal; troubleshooting relied entirely on carrier TAC with 4-hour SLA response times.

Approach: Designed a hybrid WAN architecture: critical branches retained MPLS underlay with SD-WAN overlay (Cisco Viptela), while smaller branches migrated to broadband + LTE with application-aware routing. Deployed centralized monitoring via vManage with custom Grafana dashboards for per-application SLA tracking against internal targets.

Result: Circuit costs reduced by 58% ($220K/month savings). Application availability for Citrix and SAP increased from 97.2% to 99.91%. Mean time to detect WAN degradation dropped from 35 minutes (user-reported) to 90 seconds (automated alerting).

Automated Network Compliance Auditing

Internal Tooling — Nexalink Networks

Problem: Quarterly compliance audits required manual inspection of running configs across 340+ devices, consuming 3 engineer-weeks per audit cycle. Configuration drift between intended state and actual state was discovered only during these quarterly reviews.

Approach: Built a Python-based compliance engine using Nornir for device polling and TextFSM for structured config parsing. Defined compliance rules as YAML templates covering 94 control points: ACL standards, NTP configuration, SNMP community strings, BGP prefix limits, and logging targets. Integrated with GitLab CI to run nightly drift checks against golden configs stored in version control.

Result: Audit preparation time reduced from 3 weeks to 4 hours of review. The system detected and flagged 23 configuration drift incidents in its first month of operation that would otherwise have gone unnoticed until the next quarterly audit. Now runs as a nightly automated job with Slack notifications for any deviations from approved baseline.
